## Overview

Western Rome survives into the **600s ATL**, centuries beyond the conventional OTL date of 476. When it finally collapses, it falls from a stronger demographic, territorial, and institutional base. Germania has been Roman for centuries, Atlantic colonies already exist, Christianity has only recently become the state religion, and imperial systems reach farther than their OTL equivalents.

The collapse therefore does not create the same post-Roman world. Roads, canals, cadasters, courts, urban institutions, legal records, and ideas of imperial legitimacy persist more deeply across both hemispheres.

## Causes

The sources deliberately do not yet define a complete collapse mechanism. Migration-period pressures, internal political struggles, frontier war, succession crises, and administrative fragmentation remain part of the broad historical environment, but their exact sequence is **TBD**.

The collapse should not be reduced to a single invasion or palace event unless later canon establishes one.

## Course of collapse

The following broad outcomes are locked:

- western central authority fails in the seventh century ATL;
- Britain had already been lost in the 520s–550s;
- continental successor states inherit Romanized populations and institutions;
- [Byzantium](../../polities/byzantium.md) survives as the eastern Roman continuation;
- Atlantic colonies lose imperial protection and become de facto independent;
- religious exile and colonial migration overlap with the final imperial breakdown.

The exact final emperor, capital, deposition, wars, and successor treaties remain **TBD**.

## Old World consequences

### Francia and the HRE

[Francia](../../polities/francia.md) and the [Holy Roman Empire](../../polities/holy-roman-empire.md) emerge as major western successor systems. Because Germania was integrated and the Empire lasted longer, both inherit deeper Roman administrative and political traditions.

### Britain

Britain follows a distinct path because Roman authority had already disappeared there. Celtic, Anglo-Saxon, Scandinavian, Francian, and HRE layers later produce the divided British world of 1604.

### Byzantium

The eastern Empire survives, preserving the most direct Old World claim to Roman state continuity. Its later control of the Straits and Anatolia makes this survival a living geopolitical fact, not merely ceremonial memory.

## American consequences

Colonial nodes do not vanish when central authority ends. Instead:

- Atlantic and Gulf colonies become separate successor polities;
- municipal law and clerical records provide institutional continuity;
- Indigenous powers raid and recover territory from weakened settlements;
- Caribbean islands form local governments;
- many South American settlements collapse, while a few major cities survive through outside support;
- the political conditions are created for the later formation of the [United States](../../polities/united-states.md).

## Historical significance

The fall of western Rome is not the end of Roman history in HFR. It is the **fragmentation of a Roman world** whose successor claims remain central through 1604. The setting's major states are shaped as much by competing versions of Roman continuity as by later national identity.
