## Overview

The **Franco-Roman War** is the decisive western European conflict of the late sixteenth century ATL. The [Holy Roman Empire](../../polities/holy-roman-empire.md) attacks or intervenes to check the long-standing power of [Francia](../../polities/francia.md). Fighting in the Low Countries and British world ends in a major Francian defeat.

The war destroys Francia's hegemonic position and establishes the HRE as the leading continental power. It also creates the divided British settlement of 1604.

## Background

For centuries, Francia had been the dominant western power. It controlled Gaul, a northern Hispania belt, England, Wales, and a major maritime and colonial position. The HRE remained strong but secondary until its later consolidation and industrial rise.

Their rivalry is not merely territorial. Both states claim deep western Roman inheritance and compete for the right to define imperial order in Europe.

The exact diplomatic crisis, ultimatum, alliance structure, and formal casus belli remain **TBD**.

## Belligerents

The hard-canon participants are:

- the HRE;
- Francia;
- Celtic powers aligned with the HRE in the British theater.

## Course of the war

The sources establish the broad theaters:

### Low Countries

The HRE invades or defeats Francian power in the Low Countries and gains major territory. Exact cities, fortresses, occupation zones, and campaigns remain **TBD**.

### British theater

England had previously been under Francian control. During the war:

- the HRE gains most of the former Danelaw lands in eastern and northern England;
- the Celtic Union gains or consolidates North Umbria;
- Francia retains western England and essentially all of Wales.

Older summaries use varying phrases such as “eastern and northern England” or “East England.” V3's 1604 settlement—most former Danelaw to HRE, North Umbria to Celtic Union, west and Wales to Francia—is the current baseline.

### Colonial theaters

Limited colonial fighting occurs, but the conflict is decided in Europe. Exact colonies and naval engagements remain **TBD**.

## Outcome

The HRE wins a decisive strategic victory. Francia is humiliated, loses continental and British territory, and ceases to be Europe's uncontested leading power.

The HRE emerges as:

- the strongest state in Europe;
- the principal Catholic imperial power;
- ruler of Germany, Switzerland, Austria, Bohemia, northern Italy to Rome, and most former Danelaw lands.

Francia remains a great power with Gaul, northern Hispania, western England, Wales, and an incompletely mapped colonial empire. It is wounded rather than destroyed.

## Consequences

### Revisionist Francia

Francia enters 1604 with a fresh grievance, lost prestige, and strong incentives to revise the settlement.

### HRE dominance

The HRE's power stabilizes central Europe while simultaneously alarming every state threatened by continental hegemony.

### Divided Britain

The British settlement is not a unified peace but an unstable partition among the HRE, Francia, and Celtic Union.

### Great War pressure

The war creates one of the main unresolved rivalry systems of the pre-Great-War era.
