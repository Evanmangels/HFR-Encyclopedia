## Overview

In **520 ATL**, Christianity becomes the official religion of the Roman state. The date is a hard canon lock and replaces older loose references to conversion in the 550s. The late conversion means Roman pagan institutions remain publicly normal and prestigious for centuries longer than in OTL.

The shift is not merely religious. It reorganizes migration, law, elite identity, and the geography of the Atlantic colonies. Christians had already moved toward the East Coast while the imperial center remained pagan. After 520, legal and social pressure turns against pagans, and many pagan households—especially skilled port, river, and urban workers—move toward the Gulf colonies.

## Background

Christianity grows inside a Roman Empire whose court, civic cults, temples, aristocratic memory, and public symbolism remain pagan much longer than in OTL. The sources do not yet establish the emperor, council, civil war, conversion narrative, or political bargain that leads to the decision in 520.

Those mechanisms remain **TBD**.

## The settlement of 520

The broad consequences are established:

- the imperial court grants formal legal privilege to the Church;
- temple closures and restrictions escalate;
- Christian institutions gain a new relationship with imperial authority;
- pagan elites and communities lose political security;
- migration into the colonies intensifies.

The exact legal text, pace of enforcement, and provincial variation remain **TBD**.

## Old World consequences

Christianity eventually becomes dominant across most of Europe, but paganism declines more slowly and unevenly than in OTL. Public pagan traditions survive into later medieval centuries before being suppressed, absorbed, or transformed into local survivals and syncretic rites.

The later Roman successor states inherit a deeply Christian political framework, but also stronger memories of pagan civic culture than their OTL analogues.

## Atlantic consequences

### Christian Atlantic seaboard

Before 520, Christians escaping or leaving a still-pagan imperial core contribute to the religious profile of the Atlantic East Coast. The future [United States](../../polities/united-states.md) is therefore Christian-weighted, though never originally religiously uniform.

### Pagan Gulf

After 520, pagan migration concentrates more strongly in Gulf colonies. This helps create the distinctive religious and civic tradition that later shapes Liga Caesarea and, much later, Caesar's Legions.

The result is a transatlantic religious sorting process whose political effects remain active more than a thousand years later in the [Legionary Civil War](legionary-civil-war.md).

## Historical significance

The event fixes one of HFR's central civilizational fault lines: different Roman successor societies can all claim Roman inheritance while disagreeing fundamentally over whether the truest Rome is Christian, pagan, imperial, federal, eastern, or western.
