## Overview

The **First Great Indian War**, fought from **1566 to 1570 ATL**, is the central military catastrophe of modern North American history. It begins as a coordinated attempt by the [Mexica Double Eagle Empire](../../polities/mexica-double-eagle-empire.md) and [Iroquois Confederacy](../../polities/iroquois-confederacy.md) to break the [United States](../../polities/united-states.md) at its two great industrial centers. Roughly a month later, the [United Tribes of America](../../polities/united-tribes-of-america.md) enters and helps ignite widespread revolt across the plains, Great Basin, and interior.

The USA survives, but it loses the continental empire it had built. The war establishes the modern multipolar order: the MDE, UTA, Iroquois, [New California Republic](../../polities/new-california-republic.md), [New Vegas](../../polities/new-vegas.md), and independent Great Basin powers all become permanent strategic realities.

## Background

Before 1566, the USA had reached the Pacific and held California for roughly four decades. Its expansion displaced Indigenous peoples, broke earlier agreements, destroyed or subordinated political systems, and extended military and rail commitments across an enormous continent.

The position was powerful but brittle. The federation faced:

- the MDE in the south and southwest;
- the Iroquois in the northeast and Great Lakes system;
- rising plains confederation under the UTA;
- resentment among Great Basin peoples;
- and severe religious and regional tensions inside the USA itself.

The exact diplomatic conference, alliance treaty, or immediate incident that begins the war remains **TBD**.

## Strategic objectives

### Coalition objectives

The opening MDE and Iroquois plan is aimed at the USA's industrial heartlands rather than mere frontier gains:

- the MDE attacks the lower-Mississippi and Gulf system;
- the Iroquois attacks the northeastern metropolitan-industrial belt;
- the UTA later opens the plains and upper-interior crisis;
- and Great Basin revolts destroy the USA's western communications.

The coalition is operationally coordinated, but the sources do not yet establish a permanent unified command or formal alliance government.

### USA objectives

The USA must prevent collapse of its northeastern and lower-Mississippi cores, preserve rail and river connections, hold California, and suppress uprisings behind the front. Its leadership also faces a political dilemma: resources sent to one theater cannot be used in another, and southern pagans increasingly believe the federal center is prioritizing the Christian, Atlantic Standard-speaking northeast.

## Belligerents and theaters

### Mexica southern and southwestern theaters

The MDE advances through Sonora and Chihuahua, devastates USA forces in Texia and Monterrae, attacks along the Gulf, moves through the Mojave, and campaigns in Baja. Its eastern army threatens the New Orleans-area heartland, while western forces isolate California and later besiege New Vegas.

### Iroquois northeastern theater

The Iroquois invade **Columbea and Nova Caeserea**, threaten the Great Lakes and “Michigan” approaches, push toward the Atlantic, and cut off Boralea. This is not a peripheral frontier raid. The Confederacy carries industrial war directly into two of the USA's oldest, most urbanized, and most economically important states.

The northeastern theater includes sustained battles along the dense urban, rail, and industrial corridors of Columbea and Nova Caeserea. Iroquois forces attempt to break through the metropolitan belt associated with the setting's Boston-, New York-, and Philadelphia-region analogues while other formations operate toward the Great Lakes. A western push through the “Michigan” approaches is intended both to isolate northern USA territory and, if possible, to connect with UTA forces near the Chicago-region corridor.

The Iroquois reach the coast closely enough to isolate Boralea but cannot fully rupture the largest metropolitan defenses. During the second year, the front begins to move against them. *Rome (With Colonialism)* describes this stage as resembling the OTL Western Front, though with fewer continuous trenches and somewhat greater operational movement.

The following are established: major battles occur through urbanized Columbea and Nova Caeserea; Boralea is cut off; and the Iroquois threaten the northeastern industrial core. Exact battle names, commanders, city-by-city front lines, rail junctions, siege operations, and the final sequence of the retreat remain **TBD**.

### UTA plains and upper-interior theater

The UTA enters about a month after the opening attacks. Its cavalry-heavy forces, associated with lever-action rifles, encircle a major USA army and advance toward the Mississippi valley. UTA intervention helps transform local rebellions into a continental crisis.

### Great Basin and western revolts

Apache, Navajo, Shoshone, Mojave, Ute, and other peoples revolt across the Basin. USA authority collapses, but no single replacement state conquers the region. The resulting strategic fragmentation becomes permanent.

## Course of the war

### Phase I — Coordinated opening attacks, 1566

The MDE and Iroquois launch simultaneous offensives against opposite ends of the USA. The design seeks to prevent the federation from concentrating its industrial and military resources.

The MDE rapidly defeats USA formations in the southwest and Gulf approaches. The Iroquois drive into the urbanized states of Columbea and Nova Caeserea, fight through the northeastern metropolitan-industrial belt, and isolate Boralea. The USA faces major threats to both of its principal industrial regions.

The exact opening date, declarations, first battles, and operational code names remain **TBD**.

### Phase II — UTA intervention and continental revolt

Roughly one month into the war, the UTA enters. Wider revolt spreads through the plains, Great Basin, and parts of the Great Lakes interior.

*Rome* describes UTA forces absorbing or coordinating with rebelling Sioux elements, encircling a major USA army, and opening the route toward the Mississippi valley. V3 locks the encirclement and push; the identity of the surrounded army and the precise campaign map remain **TBD**.

### Phase III — Maximum coalition advance

At the high point of the coalition offensive:

- MDE armies dominate much of the southwest and threaten the Gulf heartland;
- Iroquois armies push deep into the northeast but cannot break the largest metropolitan areas;
- UTA forces threaten the Mississippi system;
- and the Great Basin becomes effectively ungovernable from the USA center.

*Rome* develops the Iroquois front in the second year as a partially static industrial battlefield resembling the OTL Western Front, though with greater movement. This is useful operational texture, but exact trench systems and battle lines remain **TBD**.

### Phase IV — Battle for Cahokia

The UTA turns toward **Cahokia**, a place of immense sacred and political significance. Capturing it would damage USA morale and claim a powerful symbol of Indigenous civilizational memory.

Later V3 locks the following:

- the battle is long and brutal;
- the UTA assault fails;
- and the defeat becomes one of the major turning points of the war.

*Rome* develops the battle as lasting roughly six to nine months and compares its scale and destruction to Stalingrad. The duration, casualty figures, urban geography, and commanders remain **TBD** until formally locked.

### Phase V — The Gulf reversal and the Miracle on the Bayou

The decisive southern battle occurs near the New Orleans-area heartland under the USA commander still known by the placeholder **Mr C**. V3 locks the commander, region, and strategic result: the battle halts and reverses the MDE's greatest Gulf success.

*Rome* provides the detailed working version:

- Mr C is one of the few pagans in senior USA command;
- federal leaders underestimate the MDE and withhold men and supplies;
- he raises large state-militia forces but remains outnumbered roughly two to one;
- a preliminary action near the Lafayette region creates the appearance of collapse;
- USA forces retreat toward the Atchafalaya bayous;
- the pursuing MDE army enters difficult terrain;
- and an ambush from the north or south inflicts devastating losses.

This tactical sequence is the intended detailed direction and gives the battle its working name, **Miracle on the Bayou**, but the commander's identity, exact geography, order of battle, and final maneuver remain **TBD**.

The defeat forces the MDE to abandon its most ambitious Gulf objectives and retreat gradually toward the Rio Grande.

### Phase VI — Siege of New Vegas

In the west, MDE forces defeat a Californian detachment and move against New Vegas, the last major fortified and financial center across a large surrounding region.

The hard-canon framework establishes:

- an MDE siege;
- New Vegas's use of Apache, Navajo, and Shoshone mercenaries;
- support from surviving Californian forces;
- and the failure of the siege.

*Rome* adds major gold reserves as both a motive for the attack and a resource for the defense. The precise siege mechanics, decisive engagement, duration, and losses remain **TBD**.

MDE forces retreat toward southern Arizona. The Baja front remains stalemated deep into the peninsula under the working Rome account.

### Phase VII — Exhaustion and stalemate, 1569–1570

Neither side can achieve total victory. The USA recovers enough ground to save its core but cannot restore the continental empire. The MDE, UTA, and Iroquois lack the manpower and equipment to continue their deepest advances.

Internal USA tensions intensify, especially among Gulf pagans who believe the union abandoned them. Fighting settles into exhaustion and stalemate before peace is concluded in 1570.

The treaty name, signatories, commissions, prisoner exchanges, reparations, and exact date remain **TBD**.

## Settlement and territorial outcome

The hard-canon results are:

- USA control collapses across the Great Basin and Pacific interior;
- the USA loses its direct path to the Rio Grande and MDE frontier;
- the UTA retains major gains to approximately Iowa–Nebraska;
- the Iroquois secures the Lake Ontario region;
- California, having remained aligned with the USA, becomes the independent NCR after being cut off;
- New Vegas becomes a neutral city-state and Mojave rail hinge;
- the Great Basin becomes a mosaic of independent Indigenous powers;
- and the MDE remains a great power in the Rio Grande and New Mexico corridor.

## Political and civilizational consequences

The war destroys the USA's belief that every frontier is temporary. It demonstrates that Indigenous industrial powers can coordinate, attack, and permanently reverse Roman-American expansion.

Inside the USA, the war radicalizes the Gulf and interior south. Anti-pagan discrimination, resource disputes, and the memory of federal neglect lead directly to the secession of [Caesar's Legions](../../polities/caesars-legions.md) in 1575.

The war therefore produces two ruptures at once:

1. the end of USA continental hegemony;
2. the civilizational fracture of the Roman-American world.
