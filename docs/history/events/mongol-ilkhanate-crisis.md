## Overview

The Mongols remain an **OTL-scale world-historical threat** in HFR. They conquer China and Persia, impose power across the steppe and Rus lands, and drive deeply into the Near Eastern world. The principal divergence lies not in weakening the Mongol conquests, but in the political systems that survive or emerge afterward.

The Mongol era creates two of the setting's most important long-term structures:

- the **[Golden Horde](../../polities/golden-horde.md)** survives as a durable steppe great power;
- the crisis destroys the previous Persian–Mesopotamian order and creates the conditions for new Byzantine and Islamic state formation.

## Eurasian conquests

The hard-canon framework establishes that Mongol power:

- conquers China;
- topples or conquers Persia;
- reaches eastern Europe and imposes dominance over Rus lands;
- weakens [Byzantium](../../polities/byzantium.md) and the Abbasid order;
- disrupts Mesopotamia and the Levant;
- reaches much of inland Anatolia in the later current direction of *Rome*.

Exact campaigns, rulers, sieges, and dates are **TBD**.

## The Golden Horde

In eastern Europe, the Mongol legacy produces a [Golden Horde](../../polities/golden-horde.md) that does not fade into relative irrelevance. It remains a durable political and military power through 1604.

Novgorod survives by submission and tribute rather than destruction. Its cities and institutions continue operating, and it becomes an intermediary between European markets and the steppe. This preserved core later enables [Novgorod](../../polities/novgorod.md) to stop paying tribute, defeat Muscovy, and rise as a great power.

## The Ilkhanate and Near Eastern crisis

Later material in *Rome (With Colonialism)* establishes the intended direction that the Mongol invasion goes farther in the Near East than some older summaries suggested. The Mongols collapse the older Abbasid power, penetrate the Levant, conquer Persia, and take much of inland Anatolia. The Ilkhanate then controls a broad but unstable successor sphere.

The precise status of Egypt during this period—direct conquest, tributary status, or looser subordination—remains **TBD**.

## Byzantine recovery

The crisis encourages internal consolidation in Byzantium. During the weakening and collapse of the Ilkhanate system, Byzantium retakes most of Anatolia. Possible recovery in parts of the Levant remains **TBD**.

This recovery helps establish the Byzantine territorial baseline of 1604: control of the Straits, most of Anatolia, and the southern Balkans.

## Rise of the Hijez Sultanate

A new Islamic dynasty arises from the Arabian–Egyptian world and unites major Arab territories under the **Hijez Sultanate**. Its ideological project is tied to reconquest and restoration across **Dar al-Islam** after the Mongol crisis.

Current project correction establishes that by **1604 ATL**, both Egypt and the former Abbasid polity have been absorbed into the [Hijez Sultanate](../../polities/hijez-sultanate.md). Older V3 passages treating Egypt and the Abbasids as independent present-day rivals are therefore obsolete.

The exact dates of Hijez foundation, conquest of Egypt, annexation of Baghdad, and final destruction or absorption of Abbasid institutions remain **TBD**.

## Historical significance

The Mongol era prevents the eastern world from settling into a simple Persian, Islamic, Byzantine, or Rus order. Instead, it produces a lasting balance among the Horde, Novgorod, Byzantium, Persia, and Hijez.
