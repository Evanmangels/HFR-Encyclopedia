## Overview

The **Legionary Civil War** is fought from **1575 to 1581 ATL**, five years after the end of the [First Great Indian War](first-great-indian-war.md). Pagan Gulf and interior regions secede from the [United States](../../polities/united-states.md) and proclaim **[Caesar's Legions](../../polities/caesars-legions.md) (CL)**.

The USA wins only a **pyrrhic victory**. It recovers major eastern and northern territories but fails to destroy the secessionist state. CL survives as an independent pagan Roman-successor power, permanently dividing the Atlantic Roman world.

## Background

The civil war grows from several connected grievances:

- the catastrophic USA defeat in the First Great Indian War;
- the belief that the northeastern Christian-led government prioritized its own heartland while neglecting Gulf defenses;
- long-standing anti-pagan discrimination;
- resentment over the earlier forced or coerced incorporation of Liga Caesarea into the USA;
- linguistic and cultural differences between Atlantic Standard and Gulf Occidental communities;
- rival beliefs about which society represents the truest continuation of Roman civilization.

The exact political crisis, declaration, and leaders of the secession remain **TBD**.

## Initial secession

The original self-claimed Legionary territory extends broadly:

- from the Rio Grande in the south and west;
- north toward Kansas and Missouri;
- east toward Florida, Georgia, and the Appalachian region.

Florida and Georgia are mixed Christian–pagan borderlands, while pagan identity becomes stronger farther west. This initial claim should not be confused with final de facto control.

## Outcome

The USA defeats CL militarily without eliminating it. The clearest source-grounded settlement is:

- CL loses all lands east of the Mississippi;
- CL loses lands north of central Arkansas;
- CL remains independent west and south of the Mississippi and north of the Rio Grande;
- current V3 also places part of southern Alabama under CL as an important buffer for its lower-Mississippi heartland.

The precise river line, crossings, enclaves, and legal recognition of independence remain **TBD**.

## Long-term consequences

### Permanent continental multipolarity

The USA does not simply lose frontier holdings; it suffers a civilizational fracture. North America now contains two rival Roman-successor projects:

- the Christian-led Atlantic federation of the USA;
- the pagan Gulf/interior state of [Caesar's Legions](../../polities/caesars-legions.md).

### Rival legitimacy

CL claims that the USA betrayed Roman pluralism and abandoned the Gulf. The USA regards CL as a rebellion and likely maintains claims to its territory. The exact constitutional and diplomatic doctrines remain **TBD**.

### Armed peace

By 1604, the Mississippi frontier is one of the world's major flashpoints. Bridges, ferries, rail approaches, customs stations, and disputed communities turn the river into a modern militarized border.
