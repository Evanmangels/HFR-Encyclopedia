## Overview

**Persia** is the enduring Iranian plateau great power of HFR. Its survival blocks the OTL pattern of durable Islamic conquest across Iran and into Central and South Asia, preserving a major independent civilizational pole between the Mediterranean, the steppe, India, and Inner Asia.

After Western Rome's collapse, Persia retook and held Mesopotamia for centuries. [Mongol](../history/events/mongol-ilkhanate-crisis.md) and Ilkhanate conquest broke that order in the 1200s, after which Persia lost Mesopotamia and re-emerged as a plateau-centered power. By **1604 ATL**, it remains one of the major states of Eurasia, but it has fallen behind the leading western European powers in industrial development.

The exact Persian capital, dynasty, government, religious settlement, industrial level, and borders remain **TBD**.

## Name and civilizational identity

**Persia** is the established project name. The state's formal endonym and imperial titulature are **TBD**.

Persia's significance is not limited to political survival. It preserves an Iranian civilizational center strong enough to alter the development of Islam, Mesopotamia, Central Asia, and India. It is neither absorbed into a caliphal empire nor reduced to a peripheral remnant.

The exact relationship among Persian identity, language, religion, dynasty, and regional peoples remains underdeveloped and should not be invented.

## Territory and administration

### Confirmed broad frame

The current working territorial frame is:

- the Iranian plateau forms the core of the state;
- Khorasan is the principal eastern hinge of Persian power;
- most of Baluchistan belongs to Persia, except near the Indus-facing edge;
- a small but important Kabul-centered mountain buffer separates Persia from parts of India;
- the Amu Darya / Samarkand world forms the major northern and eastern frontier with settled Inner Asian khanates;
- Mesopotamia was historically Persian but is no longer Persian in the present era.

### Mesopotamia and the Hijez correction

Older V3 material described a present-day rivalry among Persia, the Abbasids, Egypt, and Byzantium. Current project-chat canon supersedes that framework: by 1604, Egypt and the former Abbasid state are incorporated into the [Hijez Sultanate](hijez-sultanate.md).

Accordingly, Persia's former western rivalry with the Abbasid Mesopotamian state is now part of a broader and still-underdeveloped **Persia–Hijez** relationship. Former Abbasid holdings in Mesopotamia belong to the Hijez state under the current correction, though the exact border and administrative arrangement are **TBD**.

## Religion and society

Persia's survival preserves broad **Iranian religious and civilizational continuity**, but the exact religious settlement remains unresolved. Older notes raise possibilities such as Zoroastrian restoration or plural toleration without locking either.

Therefore:

- a simple claim that Persia is uniformly Zoroastrian would be unsupported;
- a claim that it became an Islamic Persian state would conflict with the setting's central divergence unless carefully developed;
- the role of Zoroastrian, Christian, Jewish, local, and other communities remains **TBD**.

The state's linguistic, ethnic, and social composition likewise requires future development.

## Economy and infrastructure

Persia controls or influences major overland corridors connecting Mesopotamia, Anatolia, the Iranian plateau, Khorasan, Central Asia, Baluchistan, and India. Its strategic economy should therefore be understood through caravan routes, plateau cities, frontier markets, and the competition between overland transport and maritime routes controlled by other powers.

By 1604, Persia has not modernized as successfully as the leading western European states. Later *Rome* material presents it as somewhat technologically and industrially behind western Europe, while V3 leaves the exact degree open. The safe canonical formulation is:

> Persia is a great power that has fallen behind the leading industrial states, but the scale, causes, and consequences of that gap remain TBD.

Railways, telegraph networks, industrial districts, fiscal systems, currency, trade monopolies, and resource production remain **TBD**.

## Foreign relations

### Hijez Sultanate

The [Hijez Sultanate](hijez-sultanate.md) now incorporates Egypt and the former Abbasid state. It is therefore the principal western Islamic power confronting the Persian sphere. The exact border, rivalry, diplomatic history, and balance over Mesopotamia remain **TBD**.

### Byzantium

[Byzantium](byzantium.md) controls most of Anatolia and the Straits. It remains a major third pole in western Persian strategy, preventing the region from becoming a simple two-state contest.

### Central Asia and Samarkand

Persia's northern and eastern frontier meets the Amu Darya / Samarkand world and the settled Inner Asian khanates. The exact states, frontiers, and alliances remain **TBD**.

### Kabul and India

Persia does not simply absorb northwestern India. Instead, the Persian–Indian interface runs through Baluchistan, the Kabul hinge, and the wider Amu Darya–Samarkand system. This keeps India politically plural while allowing strong Persian cultural and diplomatic influence.

### Golden Horde and steppe

Persia's relationship with the [Golden Horde](golden-horde.md) and other steppe powers is historically shaped by the Mongol world, but the present diplomatic system remains **TBD**.

## History

### Enduring resistance to Islamic expansion

Persia remains strong enough to prevent durable Islamic political expansion across the Iranian plateau and farther east in the OTL pattern. This redirects the Islamic world's center of gravity westward and toward maritime systems.

### Persian Mesopotamia

During and after Western Rome's collapse in the 600s ATL, Persia establishes control over Mesopotamia. Persian administration shapes the region's river commerce and political order for centuries.

### Mongol and Ilkhanate conquest

In the **1200s ATL**, Mongol expansion topples Persia and breaks Persian control of Mesopotamia. The Ilkhanate-era disruption transforms the entire Near East.

### Post-Mongol restoration

Persia survives or re-forms as an Iranian plateau state after the Mongol crisis, but Mesopotamia passes into the Abbasid successor order and, under current canon, later into the Hijez Sultanate.

### Industrial-era decline

By the early modern and industrial era, Persia remains a great power but does not modernize as successfully as the leading European states. The chronology and internal causes of this relative decline are **TBD**.

## Culture and identity

Persia represents civilizational endurance without universal victory. It survives Rome, Islam's rise, Mongol conquest, Abbasid recovery, and industrial competition while remaining recognizably Iranian.

Its role in HFR is to keep the Near East, Central Asia, and the approaches to India multipolar. It is a civilizational counterweight whose continuing existence prevents any single Mediterranean, Islamic, steppe, or South Asian system from organizing the entire region.
