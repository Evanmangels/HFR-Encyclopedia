## Overview

The **New California Republic**, abbreviated **NCR**, is the independent Californian successor state created when the [United States](united-states.md) lost the ability to hold or supply its far-western territories during and after the [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md).

California remained aligned with the USA during the war, but the collapse of USA control across the Great Basin cut it off from the eastern federation. It therefore became a new Roman-American successor state rather than an Indigenous conquest zone or restored USA province.

By 1604, the NCR controls California, maintains a protected rail corridor through the Mojave to [New Vegas](new-vegas.md), and borders the Western Territory of the [United Tribes of America](united-tribes-of-america.md) along part of the Columbia River. It is one of the great western poles of North America and a likely center of future Pacific politics.

## Name and state formation

“NCR” and “New California Republic” remain project labels. The final official name, Atlantic Standard form, demonym, constitutional title, and symbolism are **TBD**.

Its creation is best understood as a forced transformation of a distant USA possession into an autonomous successor state. The exact declaration of independence, legal continuity, recognition, and relationship to the former USA state or territorial government remain unresolved.

## Territory and borders

The NCR's hard-canon core is **California**. It also maintains a protected transportation corridor across the Mojave to New Vegas.

V3 further establishes a roughly 150-mile Columbia River border with the UTA's Western Territory.

The following remain **TBD**:

- exact northern line;
- eastern frontier with Great Basin peoples;
- status of Sierra and desert communities;
- southern border and Baja claims;
- coastal islands and ports;
- and the legal width and sovereignty of the Mojave corridor.

Compatible V2 material records claims to the western Great Basin, Baja California, and broader Pacific frontage. These claims are not necessarily de facto control.

## Government and law

The word “Republic” indicates a republican identity, but the sources do not establish the constitution. Unknowns include:

- executive and legislature;
- relationship to former USA legal institutions;
- status of military emergency government after 1570;
- local and Indigenous representation;
- capital;
- judicial system;
- and the political role of rail, mining, port, and merchant interests.

## Society and language

The NCR emerges from a western USA population but develops in close contact with Indigenous Basin peoples and Pacific migration. Older project material anticipates names and communities influenced by Atlantic Roman, Indigenous, Japanese, and Korean traditions, but the exact demographic pattern is not yet locked.

Atlantic Standard is likely important among urban elites and former USA institutions, but a full language map has not been established.

Religion, ethnic composition, citizenship, and Indigenous legal status remain **TBD**.

## Economy and infrastructure

The NCR's survival depends on Pacific ports, Californian resources, and the protected Mojave link to New Vegas. Its separation from the USA makes rail and commercial access across politically fragmented territory a matter of state survival.

The sources imply:

- major Pacific trade potential;
- valuable Californian agriculture, mining, and industry;
- strategic dependence on protected overland corridors;
- and strong incentives to maintain New Vegas neutrality.

Exact industries, currency, ports, rail routes, and population remain **TBD**.

## Armed forces

California fought beside the USA during the Great Indian War. Surviving Californian forces later helped defend New Vegas after an MDE force defeated a Californian detachment and moved on the city.

By 1604, the NCR must defend:

- the Pacific coast;
- Great Basin approaches;
- the Mojave corridor;
- the Columbia frontier with the UTA;
- and possible MDE pressure toward Baja or the southern frontier.

Military organization, fleet, conscription, fortifications, and doctrine remain **TBD**.

## Foreign relations

### New Vegas

New Vegas is the NCR's essential inland partner and corridor anchor. Their relationship likely involves security guarantees, rail rights, trade, and mutual interest in preventing MDE or Basin domination, but exact treaties remain **TBD**.

### United States

The NCR descends from USA California but is now independent. Whether relations are friendly, resentful, formally allied, or legally ambiguous remains **TBD**.

### United Tribes of America

The two share a Columbia River frontier. Trade, crossing rights, and military tension remain unresolved.

### Mexica Double Eagle Empire

The MDE campaigned in Baja and the Mojave and may claim portions of the southern NCR sphere. The postwar frontier remains a major tension point.

### Great Basin peoples

The NCR cannot treat the Basin as empty territory. Its eastern access depends on negotiated corridors, local alliances, and the autonomy of numerous peoples.

## History

### USA California

The USA reached and held California during its era of maximum expansion, controlling it for approximately four decades before the Great Indian War.

### War and separation

California sided with the USA during the war. The collapse of USA authority across the Great Basin made continued rule from the east impossible. Californian forces fought the MDE and contributed to the defense of New Vegas.

### Independent western state

After the war, California became an effective independent state. V3 treats the NCR as more than a stranded province: it is a durable Roman-American successor with its own western future.
