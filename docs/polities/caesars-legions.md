## Overview

**Caesar's Legions**, commonly abbreviated **CL**, is the principal Gulf-Roman successor state of central and southern North America and the chief ideological rival of the [United States](united-states.md).

As an independent state, CL is only a few decades old. As a civilization, however, its supporters claim a history extending back more than a thousand years through the Roman Gulf colonies, **Liga Caesarea**, and the long period of Gulf incorporation into the United States.

The state's national ideology rests upon the belief that the Gulf preserved a version of Roman civilization that the Atlantic federation increasingly abandoned: religiously plural, strongly pagan, regionally rooted, militarized, and culturally shaped by centuries of interaction between Roman-descended and Indigenous societies.

To the USA, Caesar's Legions represents rebellion.

To many Legionaries, the state represents **restoration**.

They do not generally understand 1575 as the moment the Gulf ceased to be American. They understand it as the moment the Gulf became politically independent again.

## History

### Roman Gulf origins

Roman settlement of the Gulf Coast began during the first wave of transatlantic colonization after Rome reached the Americas around the beginning of the fifth century ATL.

The Gulf developed differently from the Atlantic seaboard almost from the beginning. While the Roman Empire remained officially pagan, Christian migration disproportionately strengthened settlements along the eastern Atlantic coast. After Christianity became Rome's official religion in 520, religious pressure reversed direction. Pagans facing increasing political and social pressure in the Old World migrated heavily toward the Gulf colonies.

This produced a durable religious and cultural distinction. The Atlantic colonies became increasingly Christian-weighted, while the Gulf became one of the strongest centers of surviving Roman pagan civilization anywhere in the world.

The principal Gulf states eventually included **Texia**, **Missia**, and the Monterrey coastal region, alongside other southern Roman-descended communities such as **Appalachia** and **Floria**.

These societies remained Roman-derived but developed independently from the Atlantic colonies for centuries. Their language evolved toward **Gulf Occidental**, their religious traditions absorbed local influences, and their political culture retained unusually strong regional and municipal identities.

### Liga Caesarea

Around **820 ATL**, Texia, Missia, and the surviving Monterrey coastal polity formed **Liga Caesarea**.

The Liga was both a defensive alliance and a political union. It sought to protect the Gulf colonies from Indigenous powers, renewed Old World intervention, economic isolation, and the growing political strength of the Atlantic federation.

For roughly two centuries, Liga Caesarea remained an independent Roman-American state alongside the developing USA.

Its eventual incorporation into the United States around **c. 1000 ATL** was not remembered as a purely voluntary union. The exact sequence of events remains **TBD**. The Liga may have been pressured economically, threatened strategically, coerced militarily, or incorporated through a settlement combining several of these mechanisms.

Whatever the exact circumstances, later Legionary political culture came to regard the accession as the beginning of **foreign domination of the Gulf**.

This interpretation became one of the historical foundations of the independence movement centuries later.

CL nationalism therefore usually presents its lineage as:

**Roman Gulf colonies** → **Liga Caesarea** → **political subordination inside the USA** → **restored Gulf sovereignty under Caesar's Legions**

This is a national interpretation rather than a neutral historical judgment, but by 1604 it is deeply embedded in Legionary political culture.

### The Gulf within the United States

Liga Caesarea's incorporation did not erase Gulf identity.

For more than five centuries, the Gulf existed inside the USA while preserving much of its own religious, linguistic, and regional character. Gulf Occidental remained widespread. Paganism remained socially normal. Local municipalities and regional political institutions continued to matter. The Gulf also developed extensive commercial ties to the Mississippi, western plains, Mexico, and interior North America rather than looking exclusively toward the Atlantic.

At the federal level, Gulf political interests frequently aligned with broader **federalist and pluralist factions**. They generally favored:

- substantial state and regional autonomy;
- limits on federal centralization;
- protection for pagan religious institutions;
- resistance to northeastern cultural dominance;
- and greater tolerance of linguistic and religious regionalism.

This did not produce uninterrupted rebellion. For centuries, Gulf elites participated in the federation, served in its military, traded throughout its territory, and contributed to its continental expansion. The region became deeply American while remaining distinctly Gulf-Roman.

The exact sequence of political crises during these centuries has not yet been fully canonized. There were almost certainly repeated disputes over religion, federal authority, language, military recruitment, taxation, and regional rights, but most individual conflicts and movements remain **TBD**.

By the sixteenth century, however, the balance had shifted. The federal government had become stronger. The northeastern states exercised increasing influence over national institutions. Atlantic Standard had become increasingly associated with federal administration and national culture. Christianity occupied an ever more prominent role in public life.

For many Gulf communities, the USA increasingly appeared less like a federation of Roman-descended regions and more like a state being culturally remade in the Northeast's image.

### The Great Indian War

The [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md) transformed this long-running regional resentment into a revolutionary political crisis.

The war began with coordinated offensives by the [Mexica Double Eagle Empire](mexica-double-eagle-empire.md) and [Iroquois Confederacy](iroquois-confederacy.md), followed shortly afterward by the entry of the [United Tribes of America](united-tribes-of-america.md) and widespread revolt across the western territories.

For the Gulf, the most immediate threat was the MDE. Mexica armies broke through American positions in **Texia and the Monterrey region**, threatened the Gulf heartland, and inflicted devastating losses on federal forces.

At the same time, the USA was fighting for survival in the Northeast, Great Lakes, Plains, Great Basin, and Pacific regions. From the perspective of federal leadership, this was an unavoidable problem of strategic prioritization. From the perspective of many Gulf inhabitants, it looked like **abandonment**.

Southern political memory increasingly came to hold that federal authorities prioritized the Christian and Atlantic Standard-speaking northern heartland while leaving the Gulf to defend itself with inadequate reinforcements and resources.

#### Mr C

The central figure in the Legionary memory of the Great Indian War is the commander currently known by the placeholder name **Mr C**.

Mr C was one of the relatively few prominent pagans serving in senior American command and was already respected for his military ability before the Gulf crisis. As Mexica forces advanced, he repeatedly requested additional troops and supplies from the federal government. Those requests were not met at the scale he believed necessary.

Mr C therefore relied heavily on regional military resources, including state forces, local levies, and rapidly organized Gulf manpower.

His campaign became the foundation of his later political legend.

#### The Miracle on the Bayou

The decisive Gulf battle of the Great Indian War became known as the **Miracle on the Bayou**.

The precise tactical details remain under development, but the established general sequence centers on Mr C creating the appearance of a collapsing American army somewhere in the Lafayette-Atchafalaya region. The Mexica army, believing the Gulf defense was finally breaking, pursued the retreating force into difficult bayou terrain.

There, Mr C launched or coordinated a major counterattack or ambush.

The result was a devastating Mexica defeat.

The battle saved the lower Mississippi and Gulf heartland from probable conquest and became one of the defining moments of modern Gulf identity.

Its political consequences were almost as important as its military consequences. To many Gulf residents, the lesson was not that the United States had saved them, but that **the Gulf had saved itself when the United States could not**.

That interpretation became one of the emotional foundations of the Legionary independence movement.

### Postwar crisis

The Great Indian War ended without the destruction of the USA, but the old continental order had collapsed.

The [New California Republic](new-california-republic.md) emerged in the west. The Great Basin fragmented. The UTA and Iroquois retained enormous territorial gains. The MDE remained a great power. The federal state was militarily exhausted and politically humiliated.

In the Gulf, anger became increasingly difficult to contain.

Many pagans believed they had been treated as expendable during the war. Federal centralization accelerated in response to the national emergency. Religious tensions worsened. Gulf veterans returned home with enormous prestige and little confidence in the political establishment that had commanded them. Mr C and other regional military leaders became more politically influential.

What had once been a collection of federalist grievances increasingly became a question of whether the Gulf should remain within the federation at all.

### Legionary Civil War and independence

In **1575 ATL**, large sections of the Gulf and interior south seceded from the United States.

The breakaway state became known externally as **Caesar's Legions**. The exact formal declaration, original political institutions, and final in-world state title remain under development.

The secessionists presented the conflict as the liberation or restoration of Gulf sovereignty. The USA regarded it as rebellion.

The resulting [Legionary Civil War](../history/events/legionary-civil-war.md) lasted approximately six years. It was one of the most destructive conflicts in American history. Both sides possessed modern industrial armies, railways, artillery, telegraphic communications, and substantial veteran populations from the Great Indian War.

The USA ultimately recovered significant territory and could claim a military victory. It did not, however, destroy Caesar's Legions.

By **1581 ATL**, the war ended with CL still independent.

The outcome produced one of the central historical arguments of modern North America. The USA could truthfully argue that it defeated the rebellion militarily and prevented the Legionaries from achieving their maximum territorial ambitions. CL could answer more simply:

> **The objective was independence. We are still here.**

For Legionary political culture, survival itself became victory.

## Government and politics

The exact constitution of Caesar's Legions remains **TBD**, but its general political character is becoming clearer.

CL is best understood as a **decentralized military commonwealth**.

It is neither a simple military dictatorship nor a restoration of an ancient Roman constitutional system. Regional governments, cities, municipalities, temples, landowners, merchants, local councils, and older Gulf political institutions continue to exercise genuine authority.

At the same time, the **Legions** occupy an extraordinary position within the state. They won independence. They defend the frontiers. Their commanders possess enormous prestige. Veterans play an outsized role in public life.

As a result, military authority is woven deeply into the political system even where civilian government remains functional.

The state may ultimately develop a supreme executive using a Caesar-derived title, but the exact office, method of selection, limits of authority, and relationship to regional institutions remain unresolved.

The important point is that the state is **military-dominant without being purely military-ruled**.

### Regionalism

CL remains strongly regional.

Texia, Missia, the lower Mississippi, the central interior, and the western marches do not necessarily understand Gulf identity in exactly the same way. This distinguishes CL from the increasingly centralized USA.

Legionary national identity attempts to unite these regional traditions through a shared story:

- Roman Gulf ancestry;
- pagan survival;
- resentment of northeastern domination;
- sacrifice during the Great Indian War;
- military independence;
- and opposition to American reconquest.

How successful that identity ultimately proves remains an important political question.

### Mr C and the founding myth

By 1604, Mr C has become the central heroic figure in Legionary national memory.

His reputation rests first on the defense of the Gulf during the Great Indian War and especially on the Miracle on the Bayou. Later Legionary political culture increasingly presents him as one of the founders - perhaps the defining founder - of modern Gulf independence.

The language surrounding him often carries religious overtones. He may be described as divinely favored, chosen by fortune, protector of the Gulf, defender of the old gods, savior of the lower Mississippi, or the man through whom the Gulf proved worthy of independence.

This does **not** yet mean Mr C is formally worshipped as a god or prophet.

His exact status in state cult, civic ceremony, popular religion, monuments, veterans' organizations, and official ideology remains unresolved. For now, the best description is that he occupies a position somewhere between **national founder, military savior, and near-sacred civic hero**.

His eventual real name also remains **TBD**.

## Religion

Religion is central to Legionary identity, but CL is not best understood as a religiously exclusionary state.

The dominant traditions descend from the pagan Roman communities of the Gulf but have changed enormously over more than a millennium. Modern **Gulf-Roman paganism** is therefore not a preserved museum copy of first-century Roman religion.

It is a living religious system shaped by centuries of:

- Roman civic cult;
- local Gulf traditions;
- Indigenous religious influence;
- military practice;
- ancestor traditions;
- regional sacred places;
- and new forms of ritual that emerged in the Americas.

The exact theology and pantheon remain under development. Traditional Roman gods remain important, but their cults may coexist with localized deities, syncretic practices, sacred landscapes, Indigenous influences, and regionally distinctive religious communities.

Paganism in CL is fundamentally **pluralistic**. There is little expectation that every citizen worship the same gods in the same way. Different temples, cities, military formations, families, and regions can maintain different religious practices while still participating in common civic ritual.

### Christianity in CL

Christians generally remain legally and socially tolerated.

CL does not treat Christianity itself as inherently disloyal. This is partly because Gulf society has centuries of experience with religious coexistence and partly because the distinction between “Christian” and “pagan” is not always clean.

Many Gulf Christian communities may practice forms of Christianity containing substantial local or pagan influence. Possible expressions include honoring traditional household spirits, participating in civic festivals, maintaining ancestor rites, observing local holy places, or combining Christian saint traditions with older Gulf sacred practices.

Other Christians may remain much more conventionally Christian and are still broadly tolerated.

The likely point of political tension is **proselytization**, not Christian identity. The Legionary state may restrict organized attempts to convert pagan communities, aggressively denounce public cults, or dismantle traditional religious institutions.

The exact laws remain **TBD**.

The broader principle is clearer: religious plurality is part of Gulf civilization, and no single faith should be permitted to erase it.

This forms an important ideological contrast with the increasingly Christian-nationalist political culture of the modern USA.

## Gulf Occidental

The principal language of Caesar's Legions is **Gulf Occidental**, or GO.

GO developed from the Latin-derived speech of the Gulf colonies after centuries of separation from the Atlantic world. It preserves a distinct linguistic identity shaped by conservative Romance features, long regional isolation, Indigenous vocabulary, Gulf settlement patterns, and centuries of independent linguistic development.

The language is one of the clearest markers of Legionary identity.

CL nationalists can point to GO as evidence that the Gulf was never merely a provincial variant of Atlantic America. It possessed its own language, religious traditions, political history, and regional civilization long before independence.

[Atlantic Standard](../languages/atlantic-standard.md) nevertheless remains widely understood, especially among educated elites, merchants, diplomats, military officers, border populations, former federal administrators, and people whose families spent centuries moving between the Gulf and the rest of the USA.

The state is therefore not linguistically isolated even where GO carries enormous symbolic importance.

## Culture and identity

Legionary culture combines traditions that do not separate neatly into “ancient” and “modern.”

It is:

- Roman-descended;
- Gulf-American;
- pagan-majority;
- heavily militarized;
- Indigenous-influenced;
- regionally diverse;
- and modern-industrial.

Classical Roman symbolism is common, but it exists alongside traditions that developed entirely in the Americas.

Military standards, civic rituals, temples, public architecture, family traditions, frontier customs, Indigenous influences, railways, machine guns, and industrial cities all belong to the same civilization.

The state does not view modernity and pagan Roman identity as contradictory. A Legionary officer can command artillery by telegraph, travel by railway, worship traditional gods, speak Gulf Occidental, and understand all of these things as equally normal parts of modern civilization.

## Geography and regional identity

Modern CL occupies a broad zone linking the Gulf Coast, lower Mississippi, central interior, southern plains, and southwestern approaches.

Its **current borders should be understood through the [Interactive Globe](../world/interactive-globe.md)** rather than through older prose approximations that have been superseded by newer cartographic work.

### Lower Mississippi and Gulf heartland

The lower Mississippi and adjoining Gulf regions form the historical and symbolic heart of the state.

This region contains the old center of Missia, the commercial arteries of the Mississippi, major Gulf ports, some of the most important pagan civic institutions, and the geographical core of the Miracle on the Bayou tradition.

The lower Mississippi is both CL's greatest economic asset and one of its greatest strategic vulnerabilities.

### Texia

Texia is one of the largest and most important constituent regions of CL.

Its size, agricultural potential, resources, population, military traditions, and long frontier history make it indispensable to the state. Texia also occupies a strategically dangerous position between the Gulf core, MDE frontier, interior plains, and western approaches.

### Central interior

The northern and central regions of CL lie close to both the USA and UTA.

These territories are heavily shaped by frontier fortification, railways, river crossings, military settlement, and the possibility of renewed war. They are also among the regions where Legionary, American, Plains, and Indigenous influences overlap most visibly.

### Southwestern marches

The southwestern frontier faces the MDE and the fragmented Great Basin world.

This area is less densely integrated than the Gulf heartland and is likely more militarized, locally autonomous, and accustomed to cross-border trade and raiding.

The exact internal administrative structure remains **TBD**.

## Economy and infrastructure

Caesar's Legions possesses a large and varied economy.

The state benefits from:

- Mississippi commerce;
- Gulf shipping;
- agriculture;
- cattle;
- grain;
- Texian resources;
- mining;
- rail transport;
- military industry;
- and frontier trade.

Its major economic geography is closely tied to waterways and rail corridors. The Mississippi connects the interior to the Gulf. Railways connect frontier military districts to industrial and agricultural centers. Ports give CL access to wider Atlantic and Caribbean commerce.

These same systems also create strategic weakness. The Mississippi can become an invasion corridor. Rail junctions can become military objectives. Ports can be blockaded. Bridges and river crossings can decide campaigns.

CL economic planning therefore exists under constant awareness that **commercial infrastructure and military infrastructure are often the same infrastructure**.

### Industry

CL is an industrial state, though its industrial geography differs from that of the northeastern USA.

Important sectors likely include:

- armaments;
- railway equipment;
- agricultural machinery;
- river shipping;
- ship repair and naval support;
- food processing;
- mining;
- textiles;
- and military supply industries.

Its recent wars also mean that military production occupies an unusually important place within the economy. Veterans, arsenals, railway workers, engineers, port authorities, and military suppliers form influential political constituencies.

## Military

The military is the most prestigious national institution in Caesar's Legions.

The term **“Legion”** reflects Roman institutional inheritance and national mythology rather than an attempt to recreate ancient Roman battlefield tactics.

Modern Legionary formations use the same broad industrial technologies as other major powers of 1604:

- modern rifles;
- machine guns;
- quick-firing artillery;
- rail transport;
- telegraphy;
- fortified positions;
- modern engineering;
- river transport;
- and industrial ammunition supply.

Roman titles, symbolism, unit traditions, and concepts of military civic duty coexist with fully modern doctrine.

### Strategic position

CL occupies one of the most difficult strategic positions in North America.

It potentially faces the USA to the east and northeast, the UTA to the north, and the MDE to the southwest while also remaining exposed through the Gulf and lower Mississippi.

This has encouraged a strategic culture centered on:

- defensive depth;
- fortified river crossings;
- rapid rail mobilization;
- strong regional military districts;
- veteran reserves;
- artillery;
- and the ability to shift forces quickly between fronts.

The state cannot assume it will fight only one enemy at a time. The memory of the Great Indian War makes coalition warfare an especially serious concern.

### River and Gulf forces

The Mississippi and Gulf are too important for Legionary military planning to ignore.

CL therefore likely maintains substantial river patrol forces, military engineering units, bridge-defense commands, port defenses, and Gulf naval forces. The exact organizational structure remains **TBD**.

The lower Mississippi serves simultaneously as commercial artery, cultural heartland, defensive line, transportation route, and potential invasion corridor. Few geographical features matter more to the Legionary state.

## Internal security

CL remains a young country whose independence is not accepted emotionally by its principal rival.

Internal security therefore focuses heavily on the possibility of American espionage, federal loyalism, military sabotage, foreign agents, infrastructure attacks, and political movements favoring reunion.

However, the state also faces a second internal-security problem: **the military itself is extremely powerful**.

Commanders who control veteran loyalty, strategically important districts, or large formations can become major political actors. The decentralized character of CL therefore creates a constant tension between regional autonomy, civilian government, military necessity, and fear of individual commanders becoming too powerful.

The exact internal-security institutions remain **TBD**.

## Foreign relations

### United States

The USA is CL's principal enemy.

The rivalry is territorial, historical, ideological, religious, and civilizational. The USA generally regards CL as the product of unlawful secession and retains a powerful restorationist desire to reverse the outcome of the Legionary Civil War.

CL views the same attitude as proof that American federalism was always conditional.

From the Legionary perspective, the survival of the state depends upon ensuring that the USA never again possesses the military or diplomatic circumstances necessary to reconquer the Gulf.

The border is therefore one of the most militarized and politically sensitive frontiers in the hemisphere.

### Mexica Double Eagle Empire

Relations with the MDE are deeply hostile despite their shared opposition to the USA.

Mexica forces devastated large sections of the Gulf and southwest during the Great Indian War. The Miracle on the Bayou itself is remembered as a victory over the MDE.

CL therefore has little reason to regard the MDE as a natural ally. The two powers now share a frontier and compete over influence in the Southwest.

In many respects, CL exists between its two greatest historical enemies: **the USA, which ruled the Gulf, and the MDE, which nearly conquered it**.

### United Tribes of America

Relations with the UTA are more ambiguous.

The UTA helped destroy American continental hegemony and therefore indirectly created the political conditions in which Gulf independence became possible. At the same time, it remains a powerful neighboring state with its own interests in the central interior.

Trade, diplomacy, border negotiations, competition, and military caution likely coexist. The relationship is dangerous without necessarily being permanently hostile.

### New Vegas

[New Vegas](new-vegas.md) is potentially one of CL's most useful western partners.

Its independence, strategic position, commercial flexibility, access to the Great Basin, and willingness to trade across ideological boundaries make it valuable to a state seeking alternatives to American-controlled trade routes.

Intelligence exchange, finance, mercenary networks, and commercial links are all plausible, though the exact relationship remains **TBD**.

### New California Republic

Relations with the NCR are likely cautious.

NCR shares Roman-American roots with the USA but developed independently after the Great Indian War. CL may distrust its cultural connection to the Atlantic federation while recognizing that a permanently independent California helps prevent the restoration of American continental supremacy.

The two states therefore have reasons for both suspicion and pragmatic cooperation.

## National myth

Legionary national identity is built around a particular reading of Gulf history:

**Rome created the Gulf.**

**The Gulf preserved the old traditions.**

**Liga Caesarea proved the Gulf could govern itself.**

**The Atlantic federation absorbed Liga.**

**Centuries of centralization threatened Gulf autonomy.**

**The federal government abandoned the Gulf during its greatest crisis.**

**The Gulf saved itself.**

**The Legions restored its independence.**

This interpretation is taught not simply as political history but as a civilizational story.

The state therefore does not define itself primarily as a country that **left the USA**. It defines itself as a civilization that existed **before the USA controlled it**.

That distinction is fundamental to Legionary legitimacy.

## What Caesar's Legions misunderstands about itself

Legionary ideology often describes the modern state as the restoration of an ancient Gulf political order.

This is only partly true.

Modern CL is also a product of the very recent wars that created it. The old Liga Caesarea was not necessarily as militarized, as politically unified, as ideologically pagan-nationalist, as dominated by veteran culture, or as obsessed with national survival as Caesar's Legions has become.

The modern state therefore presents itself as a recovery of the past while simultaneously creating something historically new.

Its military institutions, founder mythology, nationalized religious identity, and shared experience of independence have transformed Gulf society.

In that sense, Caesar's Legions is not simply **Liga Caesarea reborn**. It is what Gulf civilization became after surviving the collapse of American continental power.

## Greatest fear

The central Legionary fear is not merely losing a war.

It is **reabsorption into the United States**.

American conquest would threaten the entire narrative upon which CL bases its legitimacy: that the Gulf constitutes a distinct civilization entitled to political independence.

For the most committed Legionaries, losing sovereignty would therefore mean more than changing governments. It would mean proving that 1575-1581 had ultimately been only an interruption in American rule.

## Greatest desire

The central strategic goal of CL is **permanent independence**.

Beyond that, Legionary ambitions likely include:

- securing unquestioned control of the Gulf heartland;
- protecting the lower Mississippi;
- preventing American reconquest;
- establishing stable northern and western frontiers;
- gaining international recognition as a permanent great power;
- and positioning CL as the principal political center of modern Gulf-Roman and pagan civilization.

More expansive ambitions - such as leadership of a wider pagan world or territorial revision beyond existing borders - remain unresolved.

## Caesar's Legions in 1604

Caesar's Legions enters 1604 in an unusual position.

It has survived the war that created it. That alone is a profound victory.

The state possesses a defensible territorial core, modern armed forces, extensive agricultural resources, major waterways, industrial infrastructure, a powerful veteran class, a distinctive language, and a strong national ideology.

But independence remains young.

The USA has not abandoned hopes of restoration. The MDE remains dangerous. The UTA controls much of the northern interior. Regional identities remain powerful. The political relationship between civilian institutions and the legions has not yet settled into a centuries-old constitutional tradition. Mr C's legend continues to grow. Religion is becoming increasingly tied to national identity.

The result is a country that believes it has rediscovered an ancient past at exactly the moment it is inventing a new future.

To the USA, Caesar's Legions is the surviving scar of civil war.

To the Legions, the USA is the empire from which the Gulf finally escaped.

Neither interpretation is likely to disappear peacefully.
