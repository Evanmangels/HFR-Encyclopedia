## Overview

The **Iroquois Confederacy** is the principal Indigenous power of northeastern North America. By 1604, it is not a relic of an earlier age but a modern military, diplomatic, and industrial actor controlling the Lake Ontario and St. Lawrence interior world.

Its long relationship with the [United States](united-states.md) moved from early peace to displacement and war. USA expansion eventually pushed the Iroquois out of much of their former southern territory and destroyed the trust created by earlier agreements. During the [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md), the Confederacy joined the coordinated offensive against the USA, invaded Columbea and Nova Caeserea, cut off Boralea, threatened the Great Lakes system, and secured the Lake Ontario region.

The Confederacy is closely allied with [Vinland](vinland.md), making it one of the most important links between continental North American politics and the North Atlantic world.

## Name and identity

“Iroquois Confederacy” is the established project exonym. The formal endonym, preferred diplomatic name, demonym, and constituent-nation terminology remain **TBD**.

The historical Confederacy has endured centuries of altered contact, warfare, migration, industrialization, and diplomatic adaptation. Its 1604 institutions should not be assumed to match either their OTL precontact form or a European constitutional model.

## Territory and frontiers

By 1604, the Confederacy holds:

- the Lake Ontario arc;
- the St. Lawrence interior world;
- and a strategic position between the USA and Vinland.

During the Great Indian War it regained or secured much of the Lake Ontario region after pushing into the USA's northeastern and Great Lakes systems.

The exact status of shore towns, river crossings, mixed courts, border communities, and the St. Lawrence gateway remains **TBD**.

At the mouth of the St. Lawrence, V3 places Vinland on the north side and the USA on the south side. The Iroquois position lies inland and is central to the balance among all three powers.

## Society, culture, and language

The Confederacy is rooted in Iroquois peoples but may also contain or incorporate other Eastern Woodlands communities displaced by USA expansion. The exact demographic and legal position of these groups remains **TBD**.

Its language policy, education system, religious institutions, urban culture, and degree of bilingualism with Vinlandic or Atlantic Standard communities remain unresolved.

The core setting principle is that Iroquois identity survives industrialization and state modernization rather than being erased by them.

## Economy and industrialization

*Rome* describes the Iroquois as **moderately industrialized** by the Great Indian War. Their ability to launch major offensives into the northeastern metropolitan belt confirms meaningful military production, logistics, and state capacity.

Likely strategic economic systems include:

- Great Lakes and St. Lawrence transport;
- trade with Vinland;
- arms and industrial production;
- river and lake ports;
- and customs control on the interior routes.

Exact cities, railways, currency, industrial sectors, population, and trade law remain **TBD**.

## Armed forces

The Confederacy's Great Indian War campaign demonstrates the ability to:

- coordinate its opening with the MDE;
- invade Columbea and Nova Caeserea;
- push toward the coast and isolate Boralea;
- threaten the Michigan and Great Lakes approaches;
- and sustain a front against the USA's northeastern industrial heartland.

*Rome* develops the fighting near the major northeastern metropolitan areas as a partially static industrial front in the second year, with more movement than the OTL Western Front. V3 locks the broad advance and strategic threat, while the precise trench systems, battles, commanders, and operational chronology remain **TBD**.

The army's formal branches, conscription, equipment, fleet, and command system are not yet defined.

## Relations with the United States

The Iroquois and USA once maintained a peace that lasted for centuries. Later USA expansion drove the Iroquois from territories south of the St. Lawrence and transformed earlier coexistence into hostility.

The Great Indian War was therefore both a strategic coalition war and an act of historical reversal. By 1604, the Confederacy remains one of the strongest anti-USA poles in the northeast.

The exact armistice, border law, transit rights, and diplomatic relations remain **TBD**.

## Alliance with Vinland

The Iroquois–Vinland alliance is hard canon. It creates a northern strategic partnership linking the St. Lawrence interior, North Atlantic trade, and Scandinavian power.

The alliance's date, treaty, military clauses, trade privileges, and cultural effects remain **TBD**.

## Other foreign relations

The Confederacy fought beside the MDE and UTA during the Great Indian War, but a permanent alliance bloc is not yet established. Relations with the NCR, CL, Britain, Francia, or other Atlantic powers remain **TBD**.

## History

### Early contact and peace

The Iroquois survived the early Roman Atlantic era as a major northeastern power. The sources establish a later peace with the USA, but the date, terms, and political setting remain unresolved.

### USA expansion and displacement

The USA eventually broke the earlier relationship, pushing the Iroquois from much of the region south of the St. Lawrence. Eastern Woodlands peoples displaced by wider USA colonization also moved into the northern Great Lakes world, reshaping the Confederacy's wider environment.

### First Great Indian War

The Iroquois and MDE began the war with simultaneous attacks intended to damage the USA's industrial core. The Iroquois offensive carried sustained fighting into the urbanized corridors of Columbea and Nova Caeserea, threatened the northeastern cities and Great Lakes routes, pushed toward the coast, cut off Boralea, and gained territory around Lake Ontario.

The deepest advance was eventually checked, but the Confederacy ended the war with a stronger and more secure territorial position.
