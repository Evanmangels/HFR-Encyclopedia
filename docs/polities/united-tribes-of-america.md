## Overview

The **United Tribes of America**, abbreviated **UTA**, is the dominant Indigenous power of the Great Plains and continental interior. It is not merely a temporary wartime coalition or a loose frontier alliance. By 1604 it is a territorial, military, and civilizational power linking the plains, upper interior, and parts of the western continental system.

The UTA became one of the decisive actors in the [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md). Entering roughly a month after the opening MDE and Iroquois offensives, it helped transform the conflict into a continental crisis, encouraged wider revolts, encircled a major USA army, and drove toward the Mississippi system. Its assault on Cahokia ended in defeat, but the UTA retained major territorial gains and emerged as a permanent great power.

## Name and political identity

“United Tribes of America” is an established project name. Its final internal-language name, diplomatic style, demonym, and abbreviations in Indigenous languages remain **TBD**.

The name should not be interpreted as proof that all member peoples share a single ethnicity, language, or political tradition. The UTA is a confederational project whose legitimacy likely rests on negotiated unity, common defense, mounted power, and resistance to outside domination. The exact constitutional logic is not yet established.

## Territory and frontiers

By 1604, the UTA:

- holds a major Great Plains and interior sphere;
- retains gains to about the Iowa–Nebraska line from the Great Indian War;
- borders Caesar's Legions toward the Iowa–Nebraska–Missouri system;
- borders the USA farther east and north toward the Wisconsin–Lake Superior zone;
- and, through a Western Territory, borders the [New California Republic](new-california-republic.md) along roughly 150 miles of the Columbia River.

The exact northern extent, relationship to Great Slave Lake regions, Rocky Mountain frontier, internal territorial divisions, and status of Cascadian approaches remain **TBD**.

Compatible V2 material records claims toward the Rockies, Cascades, broad USA and CL plains territories, and northeastern Great Basin peoples. These are claims or spheres of aspiration, not confirmed control.

## Government and confederational structure

The UTA is a confederational power, but its institutions remain one of the setting's major TBDs. Unknowns include:

- founding date and original member peoples;
- whether the wartime confederation predated 1566 or consolidated during the war;
- the role of Sioux peoples or leadership;
- representation of plains, northern, and western members;
- central council and executive authority;
- capital or rotating meeting place;
- treaty-making powers;
- taxation and military quotas;
- status of absorbed or allied tribes;
- and governance of the Western Territory.

The UTA should not be described as a homogeneous nation-state. Its power comes from confederational coordination across multiple peoples and regions.

## Military system

The UTA is strongly associated with:

- cavalry-heavy warfare;
- lever-action rifles;
- rapid operational movement across the plains;
- encirclement and deep advances;
- and a smaller relative emphasis on conventional infantry than many industrial powers.

*Rome* suggests that trade with a Pacific Asian colonial power and with Vinland/Scandinavia helped give the UTA access to lever-action weapons while many other states emphasized breech-loading rifles. The final identity of the Asian supplier and the exact trade system remain unresolved, but the UTA's association with lever-action rifles is locked by V3.

The army's command structure, logistics, artillery, rail use, fortifications, and relationship to tribal contingents remain **TBD**.

## First Great Indian War

The UTA enters the war after the initial MDE and Iroquois attacks. Its intervention ignites or coordinates wider revolt across the plains and interior, making a contained multi-front war impossible for the USA.

Fixed campaign elements include:

- absorption or cooperation with rebelling plains peoples;
- encirclement of a major USA army in the north;
- a drive toward the Mississippi valley;
- and a long assault on **Cahokia** ending in UTA defeat.

*Rome* develops the Cahokia campaign as a six-to-nine-month urban and symbolic struggle. The UTA seeks Cahokia not merely for logistics but because of its sacred and civilizational meaning. V3 locks the battle as long, brutal, and unsuccessful; the exact duration, tactical sequence, commanders, and casualties remain **TBD**.

Despite the defeat at Cahokia, the UTA emerges from the war with major territorial gains and enhanced prestige. It proves that a coordinated Indigenous interior power can defeat the USA strategically even without destroying it militarily.

## Society and civilizational identity

The UTA embodies a modern Indigenous path built around confederation, mounted power, territorial depth, and the memory of earlier continental resistance. The older Plains Horde tradition and the destruction of Cahokia likely shape its political mythology, but the exact relationship remains unfinished.

The sources do not yet define:

- member peoples and demographic balance;
- languages of government;
- religious and sacred institutions;
- land tenure;
- urban centers;
- class structure;
- education;
- or treatment of nonmember communities.

## Economy and infrastructure

The UTA's scale and modern military capacity imply extensive trade, horse and livestock systems, arms supply, internal routes, and some industrial or workshop base. Its geography makes control of rivers, rail connections, grasslands, crossings, and mountain gateways strategically essential.

Known external connections include trade with Vinland/Scandinavia and a likely Pacific supplier of lever-action weapons. Exact exports, currency, customs institutions, industrial centers, and rail network remain **TBD**.

## Foreign relations

### United States

The USA is a principal rival. The states face one another in the Iowa–Nebraska and Wisconsin–Lake Superior systems. Their exact armistice, border commissions, and trade relations remain **TBD**.

### Caesar's Legions

CL occupies much of the former USA central and southwestern frontier and now directly faces the UTA. The precise political relationship is unresolved.

### New California Republic

The UTA's Western Territory touches the NCR along the Columbia River for roughly 150 miles. The exact river line, crossing points, and trade or security regime remain **TBD**.

### Iroquois and MDE

The UTA fought alongside them against the USA, but no permanent tripartite alliance is yet established.

### Vinland and Pacific trade

Vinland/Scandinavian trade is important to the UTA's weapons and northern connections. The full diplomatic framework remains **TBD**.
