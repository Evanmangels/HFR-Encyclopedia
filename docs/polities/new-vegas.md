## Overview

**New Vegas** is a neutral city-state in the Mojave and one of the smallest yet most strategically important powers in North America. It sits at the junction of the Great Basin mosaic, the [New California Republic](new-california-republic.md), the [Mexica Double Eagle Empire](mexica-double-eagle-empire.md), and the wider rail and trade systems of the southwest.

The city became a major political entity during the [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md). After MDE forces defeated a Californian detachment, they besieged New Vegas. The city survived by mobilizing wealth, hiring Apache, Navajo, and Shoshone mercenaries, and cooperating with surviving Californian forces.

By 1604, New Vegas is a neutral commercial and strategic hinge, the anchor of the Mojave corridor, and a barrier against any single power dominating the southwest.

## Territory and corridor

New Vegas controls a strategically important urban center and anchors a protected rail corridor to the NCR across the Mojave and tribal country.

The exact extent of its territory, defensive perimeter, water rights, rail stations, and authority along the corridor remain **TBD**.

The wider Great Basin is not under New Vegas rule. It remains a mosaic of Apache, Navajo, Shoshone, Paiute, Ute, Mojave, and other powers, towns, corridors, and contested spaces.

## Economy

*Rome* describes New Vegas as wealthy through gambling and investment by rich outsiders. V3 strengthens the broader conclusion: the city is one of the few Basin locations where wealth, law, infrastructure, and coercive force consistently produce political order.

Its likely economic pillars include:

- gambling and entertainment;
- finance and gold reserves;
- rail transit and customs;
- trade between the NCR, Basin, and southwest;
- mercenary contracting;
- and services for travelers and political intermediaries.

The exact currency, banks, ownership system, population, and scale of gambling remain **TBD**. Gambling and investment are strong Rome-derived setting direction, while the broader commercial-hinge role is hard canon.

## Defense and the siege

During the Great Indian War, New Vegas became the last major stronghold in a large surrounding region after the collapse of USA authority and defeat of a Californian detachment.

The hard-canon framework establishes:

- an MDE siege;
- support from surviving Californian forces;
- the use of Apache, Navajo, and Shoshone mercenaries;
- and the failure of the MDE attack.

*Rome* adds that the city held major gold reserves and that these reserves helped make it an attractive target and enabled its defense. The exact siege chronology, fortifications, casualties, and decisive action remain **TBD**.

By 1604, the city must maintain sufficient armed force, fortification, hired support, and diplomacy to preserve neutrality among larger neighbors.

## Diplomacy

### New California Republic

The NCR is New Vegas's primary corridor partner. Their relationship is central to western trade and security, but the exact guarantee or treaty remains **TBD**.

### Mexica Double Eagle Empire

The MDE once besieged the city and maintains interests or claims toward its approaches. Relations are likely tense, but no formal present-day status is locked.

### Great Basin peoples

New Vegas depends on relations with surrounding Indigenous powers. The city cannot control the Basin by force alone. Escort rights, water access, mercenary contracts, market privileges, and transit agreements are likely essential, but the details remain **TBD**.

### Caesar's Legions and UTA

Both larger powers possess claims or interests in the wider interior. Their direct diplomatic relationship with New Vegas is unresolved.

## Civilizational role

New Vegas demonstrates how strategic fragmentation works in HFR. It is powerful not because it governs a vast territory, but because it controls a stable node in a region where routes, water, law, and local alliances matter more than broad claims on maps.

It is simultaneously:

- a city;
- a neutral polity;
- a financial center;
- a military survivor;
- and an indispensable rail hinge.
