## Overview

**Francia** is a major Catholic Roman-successor state and the former hegemonic power of western Europe. It consolidated from the western Frankish world after the later fall of Rome and inherited Roman administrative traditions, Latin cultural prestige, and a crown closely connected to the western Church.

By **1604 ATL**, Francia remains a great power but no longer dominates Europe. Its defeat in the late-sixteenth-century **[Franco-Roman War](../history/events/franco-roman-war.md)** transferred major territory in the Low Countries and most of the former Danelaw to the [Holy Roman Empire](holy-roman-empire.md). Francia retains Gaul, a belt of northern Hispania, western England, and Wales. It also retains a strong maritime and imperial orientation, although the exact extent of its colonial possessions is unresolved.

Francia's present identity is shaped by the contrast between historical grandeur and recent humiliation: it remains rich, modern, and dangerous, but now operates in the shadow of the HRE's continental superiority.

## Name and terminology

**Francia** is the established common name in project sources. Its official endonym, royal titulature, and the terminology used for its people and institutions are **TBD**.

The state should be understood as a western Roman-Christian successor civilization, not simply as OTL France with altered borders. Its legitimacy arises from continuity with the Romanized western provinces and the Frankish imperial tradition that followed them.

## Territory and administration

### Confirmed present-day territories

In 1604 ATL, Francia controls:

- the core lands of Gaul;
- a northern Hispania belt;
- western England;
- Wales.

The current British settlement is divided among three larger systems:

- Francia in western England and Wales;
- the HRE in most of the former Danelaw;
- the Celtic Union in Ireland, Scotland, and North Umbria.

### Northern Hispania

Francia is united with, or has incorporated, northern Hispania in some form. The precise sequence of crowns, annexations, unions, and administrative arrangements remains **TBD**. Southern Iberia remains under Al-Andalus.

### Lost territories

The Franco-Roman War cost Francia:

- major territory in the Low Countries;
- most of its holdings in the former Danelaw;
- its former position as the leading power of Europe.

The precise border through the Low Countries and England remains **TBD**.

### Colonial and overseas territories

Francia is consistently associated with maritime power, overseas trade, resource extraction, and colonial activity. Older sources characterize it as one of the world's major colonial powers and suggest that it may have been especially important in South America.

However, the present project hierarchy does **not** yet lock a specific Francian colonial map. The existence of a meaningful Francian overseas empire is a strong working baseline, while individual colonies, concessions, islands, ports, protectorates, and independence settlements remain **TBD**.

## Government and law

Francia is a monarchy centered on the authority of the crown. Compatible structured material describes a long tradition of effective elite governance, Roman administrative inheritance, and close subordination of Church institutions to royal power.

The exact present-day constitution is unresolved. The following remain **TBD**:

- the ruling dynasty and monarch;
- succession law;
- the powers of representative bodies, provincial estates, and aristocratic councils;
- the administrative relationship among Gaul, northern Hispania, England, Wales, and overseas territories;
- the organization of ministries, courts, and colonial government.

The state should be portrayed as politically coherent and administratively capable, but not with invented institutions.

## Religion

Francia is firmly **Catholic**. It does not undergo an OTL-style radical Reformation. Instead, the Church remains closely tied to royal authority, and papal influence is increasingly mediated through the crown.

The loss of Rome to HRE control gives this arrangement added importance. Francia remains a major Catholic civilization without controlling the central seat of the Papacy, while its chief rival presents itself as the principal Catholic imperial power.

The exact organization of the Francian Church, minority religions, and the crown's legal authority over clergy remain **TBD**.

## Society, economy, and infrastructure

Francia is an industrialized, militarily modern great power. Its core includes major Atlantic, Channel, continental, and Iberian economic zones. Its maritime orientation and probable colonial system make naval trade, ports, shipping, resource extraction, and overseas finance important to its political economy.

The country nevertheless faces the economic and strategic consequences of defeat. It must defend a dispersed system that includes continental territory, divided British possessions, northern Hispania, and unresolved overseas interests while confronting the HRE's stronger continental position.

Population, currency, industrial regions, railway structure, fiscal system, colonial revenues, and exact commercial institutions remain **TBD**.

## Armed forces

Francia is militarily modern and remains a great power despite defeat. Its broad strategic profile is more maritime and imperial than the HRE's, with strong incentives to maintain naval power, defend Channel routes, preserve access to England and Hispania, and protect overseas trade.

This maritime emphasis is compatible older canon rather than a complete current doctrine. The following remain **TBD**:

- general-staff structure;

- fortification systems in the Low Countries and England;

## Foreign relations

### Holy Roman Empire

The [Holy Roman Empire](holy-roman-empire.md) is Francia's primary rival. The Franco-Roman War ended Francian hegemony, but it did not resolve the civilizational competition between the two western successor empires. Francia's foreign policy is defined above all by the need to prevent the HRE from converting its present advantage into permanent supremacy.

### Celtic Union

The Celtic Union supported the HRE against Francia during the recent war and now controls Ireland, Scotland, and North Umbria. Francia's continued possession of Wales and western England makes the British world a live frontier among the three powers. Their exact diplomatic and military arrangements remain **TBD**.

### Al-Andalus

Al-Andalus is Francia's principal rival in Iberia and an important competitor in the western Mediterranean. The frontier between northern Hispania and southern Al-Andalus, along with the balance of naval and colonial interests, remains underdeveloped.

### HRE-aligned and neutral powers

Francia's relations with [Scandinavia](scandinavia.md), [Poland–Lithuania](poland-lithuania.md), [Novgorod](novgorod.md), [Byzantium](byzantium.md), and Mediterranean secondary states remain mostly **TBD**. Existing sources do not support a finalized alliance bloc.

## History

### Post-Roman origins

Francia emerges from the western Frankish and Roman-successor world after Western Rome's later collapse. Its formation becomes recognizable in the **1000s ATL**, although earlier Frankish consolidation provides its foundation.

Roman administration, Latin culture, Catholic Christianity, and strong elite government allow it to become the dominant state of western Europe for centuries.

### Expansion into Britain

England was once ruled independently by a Danish king. In the **1300s ATL**, Francia invaded and annexed England and Wales. This conquest made the British Isles a major part of Francian power and set up the later conflict with the HRE and Celtic states.

### Northern Hispania

Francia eventually joins with or absorbs northern Spanish kingdoms, creating a northern Hispania belt opposed to Al-Andalus in the south. The exact chronology and institutional form remain **TBD**.

### Imperial and maritime age

Francia became a major maritime and colonial actor during the age of exploration and imperialism. The broad orientation is established, but the specific empire is not yet mapped.

### Franco-Roman War

In the late 1500s ATL—older chronology places it broadly in the **1570s–1580s**—the HRE invaded Francia to check its power. Celtic forces aligned with the HRE in the British theater.

The war:

- severely humiliated Francia;
- transferred major parts of the Low Countries to the HRE;
- transferred most of the former Danelaw to the HRE;
- enabled Celtic gains in northern Britain;
- left Francia with western England and Wales;
- ended Francia's position as Europe's leading power.

The precise treaty, campaigns, casualty scale, and postwar political crisis remain **TBD**.

## Culture and identity

Francia represents the grandeur and fragility of a long-lived western empire. Its culture is Catholic, Roman-successor, aristocratic, and historically prestigious, but its political psychology in 1604 is inseparable from defeat.

Francia should not be portrayed as collapsing or irrelevant. It remains one of the world's principal powers. Its danger lies in the combination of enduring resources, imperial memory, maritime reach, and a powerful desire to reverse humiliation.
