# Geopolitical Tensions in 1604 ATL

<span class="canon-badge status-hard">Major rivalry systems are canon</span>
<span class="canon-badge status-incomplete">Alliance blocs and Great War spark TBD</span>

The world of 1604 is not stable peace. It is an armed suspension shaped by recent defeats, incomplete borders, military timetables, and rival civilizational claims.

## Principal fault lines

### HRE–Francia

The [Franco-Roman War](../history/events/franco-roman-war.md) has made the HRE dominant and Francia revisionist. The Low Countries and divided British settlement are the most immediate territorial pressure points.

### USA–Caesar's Legions

The [Legionary Civil War](../history/events/legionary-civil-war.md) ended without reunification. The Mississippi frontier, lower-river crossings, southern Alabama, and rival claims to Roman legitimacy make renewed war structurally likely.

### USA–Indigenous powers

The USA seeks security and possibly revision after the [First Great Indian War](../history/events/first-great-indian-war.md). The [UTA](../polities/united-tribes-of-america.md), [Iroquois Confederacy](../polities/iroquois-confederacy.md), and [MDE](../polities/mexica-double-eagle-empire.md) retain territorial gains and political memories of USA expansion.

### CL–MDE and CL–UTA

[Caesar's Legions](../polities/caesars-legions.md) now occupies the old USA southwestern and plains-facing frontier. It directly confronts the [MDE](../polities/mexica-double-eagle-empire.md) around the Rio Grande and the [UTA](../polities/united-tribes-of-america.md) across the central interior.

### Novgorod–Golden Horde

[Novgorod](../polities/novgorod.md) ended tribute and became a great power, but the [Golden Horde](../polities/golden-horde.md) remains intact across the steppe. Their frontier is one of the most dangerous unresolved geometries in the world, and *Rome* indicates that war appears increasingly inevitable.

### Novgorod–Poland–Lithuania

[Novgorod](../polities/novgorod.md) and [Poland–Lithuania](../polities/poland-lithuania.md) defeated Scandinavia together, then became rivals over the Daugava, customs, railway access, frontier forts, and Livonian influence. Neither wants Scandinavia to recover the lost territories, but neither trusts the other.

### Scandinavia–Novgorod and Poland–Lithuania

[Scandinavia](../polities/scandinavia.md) retains Denmark, Sweden, Norway, the Øresund, Iceland, Greenland, and Vinland, but it lost Finland and the eastern Baltic. Defeat drove centralization rather than dissolution. The state is therefore territorially reduced but potentially more effective in a future war.

### Poland–Lithuania–Golden Horde

Poland–Lithuania's southern and eastern frontier is shaped by the living steppe system. The commonwealth's cavalry tradition is strong, but its decentralized taxation and logistics may be poorly suited to industrial total war.

### Hijez–Persia

Hijez now controls Egypt and the former Abbasid lands, while [Persia](../polities/persia.md) remains the major Iranian plateau power. The exact frontier and immediate diplomatic posture are TBD, but the civilizational rivalry is structurally important.

### Byzantium and the Straits

[Byzantium](../polities/byzantium.md) controls the Bosporus and Dardanelles, giving it leverage over Novgorod, the Golden Horde, and every Black Sea power. Closures, fees, quotas, inspection, and neutral passage rules have not yet been canonized, making the Straits a powerful crisis mechanism.

### Francia–Al-Andalus

Francia and Al-Andalus divide Iberia and compete in western Mediterranean and colonial systems. Exact war plans and alliance commitments remain TBD.

## Opposite lessons from recent wars

The northern and American systems repeatedly show that victory and defeat do not produce simple strength or weakness.

- Scandinavia loses territory but centralizes.
- Poland–Lithuania wins territory but preserves institutions poorly suited to total war.
- The USA survives its wars but loses continental dominance.
- Caesar's Legions survives a nominal defeat and becomes permanently independent.
- Francia loses the Franco-Roman War but remains a dangerous revisionist great power.

## Unresolved alliance structure

The final Great War blocs are intentionally **TBD**. Existing rivalries suggest possible alignments, but no complete alliance map should be treated as canon until explicitly decided.

Questions include:

- whether Scandinavia aligns with an anti-Novgorodian coalition;
- whether Poland–Lithuania and Novgorod can cooperate again despite the Baltic rivalry;
- how the HRE–Francia conflict interacts with the northern and eastern systems;
- whether the Golden Horde opens the war against Novgorod;
- where Byzantium and Hijez fall in the Black Sea and Near Eastern balance;
- and whether the North American wars connect directly to the Old World conflict.

## Potential crisis mechanisms

These are **story-development mechanisms**, not fixed future events:

- a Novgorod–Horde frontier clash;
- a Scandinavian or Poland–Lithuanian Baltic customs crisis;
- a dispute over Riga or Daugava railway access;
- an Øresund shipping confrontation;
- a Byzantine Straits closure;
- a Mississippi frontier incident;
- a Low Countries or British crisis;
- a Hijez–Persia border mobilization;
- or a colonial confrontation that activates wider alliances.
